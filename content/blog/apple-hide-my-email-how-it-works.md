# How Apple Hide My Email works and when to use it

Apple's Hide My Email generates unique, random email addresses that forward to your personal inbox, so you can create accounts without handing over your real address. It is one of the better-known alias systems, and it is often confused with [temporary email](/temporary-email), because both hide your address. The difference is lifespan and control: a Hide My Email address is built to keep receiving until you decide otherwise, while a temporary inbox is built to expire. This article explains the mechanics Apple documents, how to find and manage the addresses you have created, the limits that catch people out, and when a temporary inbox is the simpler tool.

## What Hide My Email is designed to do

Apple positions Hide My Email as a privacy layer for the moment you create an account. When you use Sign in with Apple with an app or website, you choose whether to share your email address or hide it. If you hide it, only that app or website can use the unique address to communicate with you. According to Apple's support documentation, Hide My Email generates unique, random addresses that automatically forward to your personal email inbox. Each address is unique to you, you can read and reply to mail sent to those addresses, and your personal address stays private. The same feature is available more broadly with an iCloud+ subscription, where you can generate random addresses on demand, for example when you click into an email field in Safari. The intent is consistent in both places: give the service a working address that reaches you, without revealing the mailbox that identifies you elsewhere.

## How the addresses are generated and forwarded

Addresses created through Sign in with Apple use the domain @privaterelay.appleid.com. Apple states that only emails sent from designated addresses by the app or website will be automatically forwarded to the verified email address on your Apple Account. That forwarding restriction is a meaningful security property: a random third party who discovers the relay address cannot simply email you through it, because the relay is scoped to the senders tied to that account. Apple also documents its own handling in fairly specific terms. It says it does not read or process the content of messages that pass through Hide My Email, except to perform the standard spam filtering required to maintain its status as a trusted email provider, and that all messages are deleted from its relay servers after they are delivered to you, usually within seconds. Treat those statements as the current documented behavior of one provider rather than an industry-wide standard, and check them again before you rely on them for anything sensitive.

## Setting it up with Sign in with Apple

The setup path is short because it happens inside account creation. When you create a new account in an app or website that supports Sign in with Apple, choose the option to hide your email address rather than share it. Apple then creates a unique relay address for that specific app or website. To review what you have created, open the Settings app on iPhone or iPad, tap your name, then tap Sign in with Apple. From that screen you can see the apps and websites using Sign in with Apple, and if you used Hide My Email for one of them you should see the @privaterelay.appleid.com address listed. On a Mac, choose the Apple menu, open System Settings, click your name, then click Sign in with Apple and choose the relevant app or website. Reviewing this list periodically is worthwhile, because it shows exactly which services hold a relay address for you.

## Managing addresses and forwarding with iCloud+

If you subscribe to iCloud+, the management options expand. Apple documents that you can change the email address used to forward messages, pointing Hide My Email at a different mailbox than the one currently receiving the relayed mail. On iPhone or iPad, open Settings, tap your name, tap iCloud, then tap Hide My Email in the iCloud+ Features section. Scroll to the bottom, tap Forward To, and choose the address you want to use. On a Mac, choose the Apple menu, open System Settings, click your name, click iCloud, then choose Hide My Email in the iCloud+ Features section and use the Forward To menu. Apple also publishes guides for creating, labeling, deactivating, and deleting Hide My Email addresses on iCloud.com, which is where you would turn off an address that has started attracting unwanted mail. It is worth visiting this screen occasionally, because it lists the relay addresses tied to your Apple Account in one place, making it easy to spot an address you no longer recognise, no longer need, or want to retire before it becomes a nuisance.

## Limits you should plan around

Hide My Email is convenient, but it has edges that matter in practice. First, it depends on your Apple Account and, for the broader creation and management features, an iCloud+ subscription. If you lose access to the Apple Account, you can lose access to the relay addresses and the mail flowing through them. Second, forwarding is tied to designated senders, which is good for abuse resistance but can mean that legitimate mail from a service that sends from an unexpected address does not reach you. Third, it is not anonymity. Apple is the relay, so it processes the routing metadata needed to deliver your mail, even under its stated content handling policy. Fourth, and most important for planning, an alias is still an address you should be able to abandon safely. If a service is important enough that losing the address would lock you out, you need a durable, well-secured mailbox rather than any single relay identity.

## Hide My Email compared with a temporary inbox

The two tools diverge on one question: do you want the address to keep working? Hide My Email is a long-lived alias. You can read and reply, it forwards into a mailbox you already own, and it stays active until you retire it, which makes it a good fit for accounts you will return to. [A temporary inbox](/blog/temporary-email-vs-email-aliases) is the opposite by design. On the service described here, mail is received directly at an address that expires at the end of the operator's configured window, with a default retention of 60 minutes and a maximum of 1440 minutes on this deployment. There is no forwarding, no account, and no long-term identity, and once the window closes the address cannot receive new mail. Attachments are available only if the operator enables a scanner and are disabled here, and outbound sending, when enabled, is limited to short plain-text messages with a footer identifying them as sent from a temporary address. Use Hide My Email when continuity matters and a temporary inbox when it does not.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Hide My Email work with any app or website?** It works through Sign in with Apple for services that support it, and more broadly with iCloud+ when you generate addresses yourself. If a service does not support the flow or rejects relay domains, it may not accept the address at all.

**Can I reply from a Hide My Email address?** Yes. Apple states that you can read and respond directly to emails sent to these addresses, and your personal address remains hidden from the recipient.

**Does Hide My Email make me anonymous?** No. It hides your real address from the app or website, but Apple still relays the message and processes the routing information needed to deliver it, and Apple documents specific content-handling limits rather than an anonymity guarantee.

**Should I use Hide My Email or a temporary inbox for a bank or crypto account?** Neither. Use a permanent, strongly authenticated mailbox for anything tied to money, identity, or account recovery, and keep relay and disposable addresses for lower-stakes interactions.
