# Gmail is ending "Send as" for third-party accounts: what changes in January 2027

Google has confirmed that Gmail will stop supporting several third-party email features, including the ability to send mail from a non-Gmail address using "Send as." The change is documented in Google's Help Center article on third-party email account support and takes effect in January 2027, following a transition period that has already started. If you rely on Gmail as a hub for a Yahoo, Hotmail, or work account hosted elsewhere, the practical details matter: what exactly disappears, what survives, and how to move your setup before the deadline. This guide summarizes Google's own documentation and turns it into a migration plan.

## What Google is actually removing

According to Google, three capabilities are being discontinued for third-party, non-Google accounts. First, "Send as" support ends: you will no longer be able to send mail from a third-party address through Gmail on the web or in the Gmail mobile apps. Second, Gmailify ends, which is the feature that applies Gmail-style spam protection and inbox organization to an external account. Third, "Check mail from other accounts" using POP ends, so Gmail on the web will no longer fetch messages from a third-party mailbox into your Gmail account. Google frames the decision as a security and maintenance trade-off, noting that it occasionally discontinues features that require disproportionate maintenance resources. Importantly, the removal applies to third-party, non-Google addresses. It is not a change to how Gmail handles its own aliases, and it does not affect mail you have already imported, which Google says will remain in your mailbox. The distinction between features that are switched off and data that simply stops updating is the most misunderstood part of the announcement, so read the scope carefully before you plan any migration.

## What is not changing

The scope of the change is narrower than some headlines suggest, and Google lists the exceptions explicitly. Access to third-party accounts through the Gmail mobile app for Android, iPhone, and iPad does not change, so you can keep adding and using non-Google accounts there. Access to Gmail from third-party clients over IMAP and POP is also unchanged, which means Outlook, Thunderbird, Apple Mail, and similar desktop clients can still read your Gmail. The Gmail API remains available for programmatic access. Finally, "Send as" continues to work for any Google-hosted identity, including other Gmail addresses and Google Workspace aliases configured for your account. In other words, the feature is not being removed wholesale; it is being narrowed to Google-controlled identities, while third-party access shifts to the provider's own app, a desktop client, or the Gmail mobile app. That means the disruption is concentrated in the Gmail web experience for external accounts, not in your ability to read mail from a client you already use.

## The timeline, in order

Google published a three-stage timeline. In Q3 2026, the "Send as" announcement and notice period began, and all affected users receive an in-product notification. Through Q3 and Q4 2026, a transition period runs in which "Send as" for third-party accounts, Gmailify, and POP continue to work, but Gmail may restrict new configurations and users are encouraged to migrate. Then, in January 2027, Gmail fully removes "Send as" for third-party accounts, Gmailify, and POP. The practical implication is that the window to act is now, not in January. If you have a workflow that depends on sending as an external address, you want to test the replacement path while the old one still works, so you have a fallback if something breaks. Google has stated it will restrict new configurations before the final removal, which means setting up a new third-party "Send as" entry later in 2026 may not be possible.

## Your options if you use "Send as" today

Google recommends three paths. Option one is to use your provider's native website or app, sending mail directly from the third-party service, for example from your provider's webmail or its dedicated mobile app. Option two is to configure a desktop mail client that connects to the third-party account over IMAP and SMTP, letting you manage multiple accounts in one place, which is Google's recommended route for business use. Option three is to use the Gmail app for Android, iPhone, or iPad, where third-party account support continues. Google also warns about third-party tools and browser extensions that claim to replicate "Send as" functionality, noting that they often require access to your email credentials or OAuth tokens, which can expose your account to security and privacy risks. Google says it cannot guarantee the safety, reliability, or data practices of those services and recommends the official options above. Testing each option against your real workflow while the old configuration still works is the only reliable way to know which one actually fits.

## If you manage this for a business or Workspace

For organizations, the transition is an administrative task rather than a personal one. Google points Workspace administrators to its documentation on adding or deleting an alternate email address or alias, which is the supported way to send from additional identities within a Google-hosted account. If the goal is a custom-domain address, Google Workspace itself is the relevant product, and Google specifically recommends considering it as an alternative to third-party "Send as." Administrators should also audit how third-party sending is used across the organization before the deadline, since a shared alias used for invoices, support, or system notifications can quietly depend on a configuration that is scheduled to disappear. Documenting which identities exist, who owns them, and which downstream systems expect mail from them turns a last-minute scramble into a routine migration. Assign an owner and a deadline to each identity so the work does not stall, and confirm that every new sending path passes your organisation's authentication requirements before you depend on it.

## Migrating without losing mail

Google's FAQ answers the questions that usually cause panic. Imported messages are not deleted: anything already pulled from a third-party account into Gmail stays in your mailbox, and the change only affects the ability to send as or sync with third-party accounts going forward. You can still receive forwarded mail: you can configure your third-party provider to auto-forward messages to your Gmail address, and Google notes this is independent of "Send as" and does not affect it. On the receiving side, POP on Gmail for Android continues to work, and external clients can still read Gmail using POP or IMAP. The one thing that genuinely goes away is the convenience of a single web interface for composing as, and syncing from, an external account. If that convenience is essential, the closest supported substitute is a desktop client connected to both accounts over IMAP and SMTP, which Google explicitly recommends for multi-account work.

## A migration checklist

Work through the following before the end of 2026. Inventory every third-party address you currently send from or sync in Gmail, including aliases you may have forgotten. Decide, for each, whether it is still needed and who should own it. If it is still needed, pick a replacement path now: provider webmail or app, a desktop client over IMAP and SMTP, or the Gmail mobile app. If you send from a custom domain, evaluate Google Workspace as a supported long-term option. Test sending and receiving through the new path while the old one still works, and verify that replies land where you expect. Avoid unofficial "Send as" replacement extensions unless you are prepared to hand over credentials or OAuth tokens and accept the risk Google describes. Finally, tell anyone who relies on that address, including customers and internal teams, before the switch rather than after it. Keep the checklist somewhere shared, mark items off as they are completed, and revisit it once more in December 2026 to catch anything that has changed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will my existing Gmail aliases stop working?** No. Google states that "Send as" for Gmail and Google Workspace identities is not affected, and the change applies only to third-party, non-Google accounts.

**Will messages I already imported be deleted?** No. Google says any messages already imported from a third-party account remain in your mailbox, and the change affects only future sending and syncing.

**Can I still receive mail from my other account in Gmail?** Yes, in two ways. You can keep using the Gmail mobile app for that account, or you can set up auto-forwarding at the third-party provider, which Google confirms is unaffected by the "Send as" removal.

**Is it safe to use a third-party extension to keep the old behavior?** Google advises caution, because these tools often require your email credentials or OAuth tokens, and it cannot vouch for their safety, reliability, or data handling.
